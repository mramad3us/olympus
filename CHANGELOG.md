# Changelog

One entry per release, newest first: what changed for the player, and the numbers that moved. Write it under Unreleased as the work happens; `tools/release.sh` stamps it with the version and the date. An entry holds no `## ` heading of its own (the entry would end there): bold lead-ins and nested bullets instead.

## Unreleased

## v0.0.1 (2026-11-05)

- **The method, carried over from tradecraft and longhaul** (CLAUDE.md): the play copy (`.play/`, a git worktree pinned to a release tag) served by `./play.sh` on :8200 and moved only by `tools/promote.sh` on the user's go, with `--status` and rollback to an older tag; the working tree on :8201 (`./play.sh --dev`); on Windows, `play.cmd` (through `tools/play.ps1`: play, `--dev`, `--promote [vX.Y.Z]`, `--status`, the tags fetched from GitHub first). The ports keep the saves apart: one browser origin each, the gallery on :8212, the browser tests on :8223, agents' smoke runs on :8230 to :8239.
- **Releases** through `tools/release.sh`, on OLYMPUS's numbering (the user, 2026-11-05: Claude cuts patches and bumps the minor at tested milestones; 1.0 is the user's call): `patch` (0.0.1 to 0.0.2), `minor` (0.0.9 to 0.1.0), `rc --major` (opens 1.0.0-rc1), `rc` (the next candidate), `major --user-validated` (1.0.0-rcN to 1.0.0). It refuses an empty Unreleased, an existing tag, a branch other than `main`, and any untracked file outside the shipped top-level paths; it backs VERSION and CHANGELOG.md up in `.run/`, runs `npm test` (`QUICK` and `SKIP` pass through), and puts both files and git's index back if the tests, the commit or anything else before it fails. Then the commit `vX.Y.Z: <summary>`, an annotated tag, and the push to github.com/mramad3us/olympus. Checked in a scratch repository: 14 version transitions and 10 refusals, and each safeguard (failing tests, a failing commit, a stray file, Ctrl+C, a push that fails).
- **The file server** (`tools/serve.py`): `Cache-Control: no-store` (a cached module mixed with new ones fails the load), a listen queue of 128 (the standard 5 resets the odd module request), bound to 127.0.0.1, file types set in code (Windows' registry can serve `.js` as text) for scripts, images, fonts and sound.
- **The test runner** (`node tests/run.mjs [filter]`, `npm test`, `npm run test:quick`): `{ name, fn, heavy?, timeoutMs? }` per test; `QUICK=1` leaves out the heavy ones, `SKIP=a,b` names; a test that hangs is stopped after five minutes (`TIMEOUT_MS`), and a test file that doesn't load is a failure, not a crash. `tests/browser.mjs`: `withGame(fn, { port, query })` serves the tree on a port of its own, drives the system Chrome headless on the GPU (playwright-core 1.64.0), waits for `window.__ready`, and fails on page errors, console errors and HTTP errors (at once, when they come before the page is ready).
- **The rules of the code, checked:** every module in `src/` parses and opens with a header that explains it; no `Math.random` in `src/`; the sim (`src/sim`, `src/core`) never touches the DOM or three.js, and the two core modules that talk to the browser on purpose (`src/core/store.js`, `src/core/input.js`) are named and imported by nothing else in the sim; no em dashes in `src/`. `tools/shot.mjs` screenshots a page in headless Chrome and fails on its errors. `tools/awake.sh` keeps the Mac awake on a lease.
- **The game is designed end to end** (`docs/DESIGN.md`, the hub, and twelve sections in `docs/design/`, about 390,000 words): the setting of 2351 for a marine (Base Melas at −4.5 km and 65.9 kPa in Melas Chasma, the Corps from fire team to division, the Jovian Accord and the Europa stabilisation force), time (Mars sols with the 39 m 35 s timeslip, skips with splashes and interrupts, chapters), the body (air by inspired O₂, water, food, hygiene, bladder and bowel, G-load and G-conditioning, radiation, wounds and healing), skills, weapons, combat from the squad to ship combat seen from a couch, people with lives, the career, the places, the views and controls, the tech, and the roadmap to 1.0. The career opens on Monday, Sol 590 of MY 211, at 12:40 Melas time.
  - Written by eleven section writers and a roadmap writer, read by five critics (consistency, realism, fun, feasibility, your tastes), then corrected: 240 findings (24 high), 225 applied.
  - The hub now quotes your brief whole, and takes the critics' hub findings: **the first standby is lived sol by sol at 1:1** (your brief: "full days (Mars days not 24h) moving in real time until we do things that spend time like eating"), the air table by inspired O₂ (Olympus Mons' summit: unconscious in under a minute), the warmth of the thin CO₂ air explained (perfluorocarbon plants), and your carried-over tastes from LONGHAUL and tradecraft listed in one place.
  - No image generation, ever: every image and sound is drawn by the game's code (your words, 2026-11-05).
- **The engine core runs** (`src/core/`): game time in integer milliseconds from LONGHAUL's epoch (2351-03-22 08:00 UTC), a fixed 20 Hz tick (`TICK_MS` 50) over the browser's frames, at most 5 ticks a frame, with fast, per-minute and hourly layers.
  - **Three clocks from one time:** Mars local time with the timeslip after midnight (the Allison and McEwen Mars Sol Date; Mars24's worked example to 0.0001 sol), the Mars Year (Clancy et al.), and UTC as ship time, all in whole milliseconds with no Date object (checked against Date on 20,000 random times from 1600 to 3000).
  - **Skips** move a minute at a time, run the layers they cross and stop on an interrupt: a week-long skip steps 10,080 minutes in 0.5 ms.
  - **Saves:** plain JSON in IndexedDB (`olympus`), keys sorted so equal games give equal bytes, `$` keys stripped, export and import as a file; Standard (save anywhere) and Ironman (one autosave) slots; an autosave every 30 game minutes and on leaving the page.
  - **Randomness** from tradecraft's seeded sfc32 (bit for bit), in named streams so one system's draws never move another's rolls; cosmetic choices by hash.
  - **Keys** by physical position, labelled AZERTY; the binding table is data.
  - **The page:** the title paper (Continue, New career: enlist, Load a save file), the enlistment paper (Standard or Ironman), the Esc menu and the `?` keys sheet. A stub sandbox (`?play=sandbox`) walks a marker on a 1 m grid with Z Q S D and shows the three clocks; the stage replaces it in a coming patch. `window.__olympus` is the debug handle.
- **The pixel stage is ported from tradecraft and made cheaper** (`src/render/`, `src/art/`): an orthographic camera looking north at 45°, every pixel snapped to the palette, outlines from part IDs, toon light with 10 lamp-pool slots, cutaway walls (north and side walls 2.8 m, the rest cut to 1.0 m), the fog of awareness, and seven moods (day, dusk, night, ship's normal light, battle red, emergency amber, blackout).
  - One scene pass writes colour and part IDs together (WebGL2 multiple render targets), where tradecraft drew every mesh twice; the palette snap reads a lookup baked on the GPU (the same answer as tradecraft's full loop on 99.97 to 100% of cells, at a thirtieth of the work).
  - An area's walls, floors and furniture batch into one draw: a furnished room draws in 5 calls a frame (tradecraft's flat: 821), 0.35 ms a frame at 24 px/m.
  - Two densities from one constant: 16 px/m at 640 × 360 and 24 px/m at 960 × 540 (`?ppm=16|24`, default 24 until you choose).
  - **Figures** in two proportions for you to choose from: A, tradecraft's chibi (2.4 heads), and B, a taller soldier (3.9 heads); seven outfits (utilities in the Valles pattern, NCO, PT kit, the light vac suit sealed or open, the plate carrier, a Europa miner); idle, walk, run, the low-g bound, aim, fire, kneel, prone, the magazine out and in, sit, the crash couch, asleep, float and tumbles, in four facings (the left view a true left, the rifle in the right hand). Drawn by three instanced calls from one 4096² atlas, painted in budgeted bands.
  - **The kit:** 40 kinds of furniture in 115 makes (racks, lockers, mess lines, showers, stalls, gym racks, centrifuge gondolas, range booths, suit lockers, airlocks, hatches, crash couches), ten painted materials, the valley floor's ground with rover tracks and boot prints, and air weather (haze, dust storms by optical depth, steam).
- **The look round is ready for you to score** (`./play.sh --gallery`, or `play.cmd --gallery`, then the stars and scores; "Copy my picks" gives the text to paste back): five scenes, each a 2 × 2 of 16 or 24 px/m by chibi or soldier figures, differing in nothing else.
  - L1 the squad bay at reveille (13 marines), L2 the head in the evening (a shower behind its curtain), L3 the mess hall at noon (25 people in the line and at the tables), L4 the valley floor in vac suits by day and in a τ 4 dust storm, L5 a frigate's deck 5 at battle stations, strapped into crash couches.
  - Known to look at: the faint ceiling-lamp pools read as blotches on the floor in L1 and L3, and a body lying head north reads like one standing (L5's couches).
  - `./play.sh` and `play.cmd` take `--gallery` (port 8212, so the gallery's stars never mix with your saves).
- Tests: 115 in 20 suites, all passing (101 s with the heavy ones). run (the runner), syntax (parsing and the four rules, with a self-check of the code reader), version (VERSION, the Unreleased section, the entries newest first ending at VERSION), browser (heavy: the game boots from the title with no errors and `window.__olympus` answers; a save loaded and run on 400 ticks matches the original byte for byte); core (rng, calendar, loop, state, store, input, and the page in Chrome); render (palette, scale, moods, lamps, the batch); figures (art, doll, and the renderer in Chrome); kit (makers, materials, terrain); samples (the matrices, the casts, every shot crisp and on its palette, the gallery's stars surviving a reload).
