# ROADMAP: from the engine to 1.0

The milestones of OLYMPUS from 0.0.x to 1.0: what each is for, what it builds and in which order, the moments the user must be able to play when it is released, the tests and bots that prove it, and what could go wrong. It also sets the method every milestone follows (scored test scenes first, then joining; acceptance moments with tests of their own; the content edge and its save), and it reconciles the milestone numbers the other sections proposed. DESIGN.md is the hub and wins where the two disagree. The other sections own their systems; this one owns only when each part is built.

Written 2026-11-05 from DESIGN.md, every section in `docs/design/` as it stood that day (each read for its summary, build order, tests, cross-references and open questions), CLAUDE.md, and the research notes on how tradecraft reached its first playable (`docs/research/tradecraft-design-3.md` §5, §14 and §16; `tradecraft-design-1.md` §0 and §17; `tradecraft-method.md` §15). The user's words are quoted with their dates; my own decisions are marked **(my call)** and hold until the user overrules them. Sizes in patches are my estimates, made from the measured line counts of each scene's ports (recounted in `../tradecraft` and `../longhaul` on 2026-11-05; TECH §12.3 and §12.4 give the same counts), and nothing more.

Revised the same day after five critics reviewed the whole design (my calls, each marked where it lands): 0.1 is cut to a core career, with the rest of the standby as 0.1.x patches and fire, storms and the live world at 0.2 (§5); every scene is one core loop and stands at its final place on the Melas site plan, which is walked by a bot from 0.0.3 (§2.1, §4.1); pacing is measured as dead air and decisions per real minute (§2.3); moment tests run in full in node and on a test clock in the page, inside budgets, from generated fixtures (§2.3); the edge obligation is N−1 to N with one chain test, and the edge waits for the rack (§2.6); parallel rounds start with the lead's pre-registration (§2.7); and a join made before the user's score is provisional, the minor waiting for every score or a waiver (§2.2, §2.4).

Revised 2026-11-06: the user's two rules of 2026-11-05 (§1: places authored and the same in every career, events and missions procedural; every person generated) through the milestones' rows; the patch-number note after v0.0.2 (§4); the walk test as v0.0.2 measured it (§4.1); the standby's stand-ins left to CAREER §5.4 (§5.2); moment 1 on CAREER §5.2's three stops (§5.3). Revised again for v0.0.4 as built: the test scenes and the score card (§2.2), PLAYTESTS.md begun (§2.9), the 0.0.4 and 0.0.5 rows shipped as v0.0.3 and v0.0.4 (§4, §4.1), moments 3 and 4 as played (§4.4).

## 0. The road in one page

- **Ten milestones.** 0.0.x lays the foundations, walks a training sol over Base Melas's plan before any building is drawn, and builds the core of the standby week as small scored test scenes, then joins them. **0.1.0 is the first playable: a core standby week** at Base Melas, from the arrival and the check-in through the sol's routine, the squad and the range to the warning order. **The 0.1.x patches fill the week**, one scored scene at a time: the Tank, the G-house, the lane, the mat, the patrol, the ROE lane, office hours, the last roll call (my call, §5.1). Each later minor carries the same career one stage further, in the order a recruit lives it: the move and the voyage, with the live world (0.2), a Europa stabilisation tour (0.3), ships at war on the way home and the first dwell (0.4), boarding and EVA at sea (0.5), reconnaissance (0.6), the Raiders (0.7), the long career (0.8), breadth and polish (0.9). 1.0 is the user's call.
- **Four stages in every milestone**: a short brief in the chat; test scenes, one per core loop, each released in a patch and scored by the user (tradecraft's rule); joining, patch by patch, into the career (a join made before its score is provisional, and says so); hardening. Then Claude cuts the minor, once every scene of it has the user's score or their waiver.
- **Acceptance moments.** Each minor names three to five moments the user must be able to play from start to end, both in a career and from a direct start. Each has a moment test: the whole stretch played in node by the real sim and its bot, and the beats that need a picture played in the real page on a test clock, inside a budget. Each prints its timeline with its dead air and its decisions per real minute. The moments of every earlier milestone stay green until 1.0.
- **The content edge.** Every release has a last moment its career supports. Reaching it, in the rack after taps with the kit stowed, saves the career in an edge slot, and the next minor plays on from that second. It is the one save carried across versions on purpose: each minor loads the previous minor's edge, and one chain test tries every older one.
- **The order** follows the first career, builds each engine once at the shape 1.0 needs and fills it as the places need it, trains every combat system in a harmless form before it goes live, brings one new kind of place per milestone, and measures the technical and pacing risks first. Europa comes before ship combat (my call; open question 1).

## 1. What the user asked for, and the rules this roadmap obeys

The brief and the kickoff (the user, 2026-11-05):
- "Build from the ground up with functional test versions and a promotion script to test the latest stable version (like tradecraft)" (quoted in `tradecraft-method.md` §15 and in CLAUDE.md).
- "Claude cuts patches and bumps the minor at each tested milestone. 1.0 is the user's call" (CLAUDE.md), and "also do keep the game up to date on github please".
- The working arrangement of the same day: build autonomously over days toward a full, debugged 1.0, ask questions along the way, announce each new release in the chat.
- "game world is handcrafted, events and world politics, assignments, opportunities, mission details are all procedural" (2026-11-05). Every milestone's places (Base Melas, *Testudo*'s decks, the FOB and the towns, the target hulls, the special-operations settings) are authored once and the same in every career, so a place's tests validate its authored data, never 200 rolls of it; what each milestone adds that varies (incidents, offers, orders, missions and where in a place they happen) is drawn from the world's seed.
- "All people generated" (2026-11-05, asked whether the persistent cast should be handcrafted). Every milestone's people come from PEOPLE's generator, from the 0.0.9 roster on; no milestone writes a character.
- The career's path, in the brief's own order: "You start as a marine on Mars, out of basic training and train on Mars on standby for a week or two, with the everyday training looking like what it should be, full days (Mars days not 24h)" (0.1); "getting on a drop ship to be deployed for peace keeping on Europa" (0.2, 0.3); "Features should include, ship combat, ship boarding, combat on planetary grounds etc." (0.3 to 0.5); "the player can also become part of special operations teams [...] dropped in much different special forces settings" (0.6, 0.7); "There should always be a base where we go back to do things like eat and shower at the end of the day" (every milestone).

Carried from tradecraft:
- "Each mechanic is built first as a playable test the user scores, before the mechanics are joined" (tradecraft's DESIGN, Q161, 2026-10-26). The look is found the same way, from numbered samples scored by aspect (2026-10-25 and 10-26).
- The user's three worries, "clunky mechanics, an inconsistent experience, empty gameplay" (2026-10-26). The scored scenes answer the first, joining in the career's own order answers the second, and the acceptance moments answer the third.
- "the point of bringing the tutorials in the career was to have consistency" (tradecraft rc30). Test scenes stay outside careers; the career teaches through its own Plan of the Day (CAREER §5).
- On heavy changes: "tell me before starting to work on it" (2026-11-04, of tradecraft's 0.2). Every milestone opens with a brief in the chat.
- Old saves are kept "only where it comes naturally" (2026-10-26).
- The tastes each milestone's moments are checked against: no fast travel that skips play (rc50); no abstraction where an action could be lived (2026-10-27); "time should feel like a currency" (2026-10-27); "we only know what we know" (2026-10-26); nothing popping in or out of view (the user's report from Windows, 2026-10-26); a splash and a reflex chance before a big outcome lands (2026-11-04); keys by physical position with AZERTY labels; every roll readable in the log; deep, stateful gear (2026-11-03); NPCs with real destinations (2026-10-26); "at least three more kinds of everything" (undated) and "insane variety in mission descriptions" (rc52).

## 2. The method: how every milestone is built

### 2.1 Four stages

1. **The brief.** Before a milestone's first patch, Claude posts in the chat, in under 200 words, its goal, its acceptance moments, what is in and what is left out, and the order of its test scenes. Work starts at once; the user may reorder or cut (my call, after "tell me before starting", 2026-11-04).
2. **Test scenes.** Each new mechanic is first a small playable scene with its own kit and goal, reachable from the title's **Tests** menu and from `?play=test-<id>&seed=<n>`. It ships in a patch and the user scores it (§2.2).
   - **One core loop a scene** (my call, after the critics' finding that a score of 50 on "pace" cannot say which of twenty systems to rework). A mechanic too big to judge on three aspects is split: its core loop is the first scene, and what joins onto it comes in scenes of its own (the range is three: handling at 25 m, the line at distance, the armoury window and cleaning; §4.2).
   - **A scene stands where the career will use it** (my call). Its building, deck or ground is built as its final template at its final place in its site (`melas@1` for 0.1), the rest of the site stands as footprints laid out from the plan (§4.1), and the scene starts next to its building. So a join adds no geometry, and no golden hash or walking distance changes between the scene and the career.
   - Scenes are worlds of their own: they never write a career, the profile or the memorial (my call).
3. **Joining.** Scored mechanics join the career in the order a recruit meets them. Each joining patch either moves the content edge (§2.6) or fills the career before it, and says in CHANGELOG.md where the edge now stands.
4. **Hardening.** The milestone's bots and moment tests run their full spans, budgets are measured, the docs are brought true. Then the minor (§2.4).

The stages overlap: while the user scores one scene, the next is being built, and joining starts as soon as the first scored scene has a place in the career.

### 2.2 Test scenes and the score card

- **A scene holds** a start state (a place, a time of day, kit, people, the body's state), a goal line shown on arrival ("Make ready, fire a string at 25 m, clear the stoppage, unload and show clear"), the full game's rules and keys, the debug card when debug is on, and an end card with **Again** (the same seed or a new one) and **Score**.
- **The score card** (my call; VIEWS draws it in its card chrome, TECH stores it): three aspects named for the scene (at most five where a scene cannot be split further), each scored 0 to 100 in steps of 5, a ★ for "join it as it is", and a free note. **Copy my scores** puts one plain line on the clipboard for the user to paste into the chat:

  ```
  OLYMPUS v0.0.7 · test-range · seed 7 · far window 60 · shot at distance 85 · range safety 70 · ★ no · note: the dummy round never fed
  ```

  Scores also stay in `localStorage['olympus.scores']` on the origin where they were given. It is the gallery's "Copy my picks" (tradecraft `tools/gallery.mjs`) applied to mechanics.
- **What a score does** (my calls): a scene with an aspect under 60 is reworked on its lowest aspect in the next patch (tradecraft's figures went from 35 to 70 that way, 2026-10-25); a scene with every aspect at 70 or more, or a ★, joins as it is.
- **A join before its score is provisional.** A scene not scored by the time its join is due may join on Claude's own playtest (§2.9), but CHANGELOG.md marks the join provisional ("joined provisionally: not yet scored"), the release's announcement names it and asks for its score, and a score that comes later still leads to patches. Patches never wait for a score. The minor does: every scene of the milestone must have the user's score or their explicit waiver before it is cut (§2.4, item 6). The user's method is "Each mechanic is built first as a playable test the user scores, before the mechanics are joined" (tradecraft's DESIGN, Q161, 2026-10-26; CLAUDE.md), and the autonomy of 2026-11-05 is leave to build without asking, not leave to skip their score.
- **Complaints** in a note become dated rules: DESIGN.md's decision log first, then the owning section (CLAUDE.md).
- **After joining**, a scene stays in the Tests menu as a regression scene and a practice range. A scene the career has made redundant may be retired with a CHANGELOG line (my call).
- **As built in v0.0.4** (`src/data/scenes.js`, `src/sim/scenarios/tests.js`, `src/ui/cards/tests.js`, `end.js`, `score.js`; the hud role's calls). The title has a **Tests** entry listing the scenes, `test-shower` ("Ten minutes under the shower") and `test-night` ("The klaxon at 02:30"); from the menu a scene starts on a random seed, from `?play=test-shower` or `?play=test-night` on 48213 unless `&seed` says otherwise. A scene shows its goal for 8 s in `TOASTS`, saves nothing (only its scores are kept), and ends on its `endOn` fact (the shower's end; the drill's stand-down) once the splash is done; a cut activity, its fraction under 1, does not end it. **The end card waits `END_HOLD_S` 6 s of live play after the splash, or Enter** ("Enter: end the scene" on the line, C and J opening over the world meanwhile; the review of v0.0.4: the card had covered the landing, so moment 3's "back ... and clean" was never seen), then offers Again with the same seed, Again with a new one, Score and the title. The score card has the scene's three aspects from 0 to 100 in fives, a ★ and a note; **Copy my scores** gives `OLYMPUS v0.0.4 · test-shower · seed 48213 · splash 85 · vignette 70 · pace 60 · ★ no · note: …`, an empty note reading "note: none", and `localStorage['olympus.scores']` keeps `{ v: 1, scores: [{ scene, version, seed, aspects, star, note, atIso }] }`, the last 200.
- **As built in v0.0.5** (`src/data/scenes.js`, `src/sim/scenarios/handling.js`, `src/ui/cards/end.js`): a third scene, `test-handling` ("Show clear", `?play=test-handling`, with `&setup=hot` or `&setup=bench` for the tests and the bench), its aspects **the steps**, **the shot and its log** and **pace**, its goal "The 25 m line, on the tower's commands: make ready, fire your string, clear the stoppage, unload and show clear." It ends on the range's `clear` or `stopped` (`endOn` takes a list of phases), the scene's end leaving stop motion and slinging the rifle so the end card's 6 s of play can pass (the build sheet 0.0.5 §8 item 25); the end card shows the session's summary ("Clear at 09:01 · 58 of 75 · 5 V · no faults", or "Stopped: uprange · 3 faults"), **opens on Score it and takes no Enter or Space for its first 0.4 s** (the design review's: Enter, which the hint itself asks for, had pressed Again and thrown the run away). **`test-night` is refined on the user's scores of v0.0.4** (night 80, klaxon 80, drill 60: the rack's pace slower, R's lie-down in sight, the squad rising while you do, the eyes adapting to the dark; moment 4 in §4.4 and DESIGN.md's log, 2026-11-06), ready to be scored again.
- **As built in v0.0.6** (`src/data/scenes.js`, `src/ui/cards/tests.js`): a fourth scene, **`test-handling-mixed`** ("Show clear, mixed magazines", `?play=test-handling-mixed` or `?play=test-handling&setup=mixed`), test-handling with a fourth magazine of tracer whose kind is not told, scored apart on the same aspects (WEAPONS §11.3). The end card names strings sat out and over-fired ("Clear at 09:02 · 70 of 75 · 7 V · 1 string not fired · 2 faults"). The scores of v0.0.5 stand: `test-night` 100, 100, 95 ★ and `test-handling` 100, 100, 100 ★ (the user, 2026-11-07, docs/PLAYTESTS.md); v0.0.6's changes to test-handling keep what was scored (the range bot's course unchanged to the tick).

### 2.3 Acceptance moments

- **A moment** is a named stretch of play, 3 to 30 real minutes long, that the user must be able to play from start to end **in a career** at the milestone's minor. Moments are named as the player will feel them ("Clear at the window"), after tradecraft's "three moments it must have" (Q160: "The grey coat", "Two seconds on the platform", "The pitch").
- **Two ways in.** In a career, at its sol and hour. Or directly, from the Tests menu or `?play=moment-<id>&seed=<n>`, which loads a **moment fixture**: a career the game itself played up to that point, saved and committed to `tests/fixtures/` (the play copy is a checkout of a tag, so what it loads must be committed: CLAUDE.md). A direct start is therefore the career's own state, never a staged one. Its world is marked `moment`, and it never reaches the profile or the memorial.
- **Fixtures are generated, never edited by hand** (my call, after the critics' finding that a hand-made or one-off fixture goes stale with every golden change). `tools/fixtures.mjs` (TECH §14 to adopt) makes every moment fixture from its recipe `{ id, seed, start, untilMs, policy, bots }` with **`autoplay(state, untilMs, policy)`**, which TIME and CAREER are asked to specify (§18):
  - TIME's chapter engine runs every stretch that can be spent, taking the policy's default for each day card, block and free slot (spend it, or carry on with the policy's allocation: TIME §9.7 and §10.3, the character's own care automatic);
  - forced days and the stretches the policy marks as lived (CAREER's key days, the first time of each block, TIME §9.3) are played tick by tick by the milestone's bots (`newjoin`, `recruit`, `rangebot`, …);
  - every card and conversation met has the policy's answer by card id, and a card with no answer stops the generation with its id, so a new card cannot slip by unanswered.

  Every fixture of a milestone regenerates from its seed in at most 5 minutes of node compute (printed).
- **Stamps.** Each fixture carries `{ version, genDigest }`: the version that made it, and the digest of what shapes it (its recipe, `SAVE_SCHEMA`, and the goldens the career reads: the Melas site, the roster, career sol 1's (Tuesday's) schedules, the templates' `@n`). A moment test that finds a stale stamp regenerates the fixture into `.run/fixtures/` (ignored), plays on that, and prints that the committed one is stale. The quick test `fixtures-fresh` reads the stamps only (well under a second) and fails until `node tools/fixtures.mjs` rewrites the committed fixtures, which CHANGELOG.md then says, as for a golden hash (CLAUDE.md). Every minor regenerates them all. Only the edge-save fixtures (§2.6) stay frozen: they are the proof that an old career carries over.
- **A moment test** (`tests/moment-<id>.test.mjs`, heavy) has two parts (my call, after the critics' finding that 3 to 30 minutes at 1:1 in a browser, for every moment of every milestone, could never fit TECH §13.7's ten minutes):
  - **In node, in full.** The moment's whole stretch from its fixture, through `tests/game.mjs` with the real sim and the moment's bot (TECH §13.2). It asserts the moment's must-holds, records the bus's facts, prints the moment's timeline (real seconds and game minutes per beat, skips and their gaps, interrupts, rolls with their sums, the body's bands, the numbers of each rule under test), and writes a save at each beat into `.run/moments/<id>/`. A 30-minute moment is 36,000 lived ticks, about 2 s at TECH §13.2's 50 µs a tick, plus its skips.
  - **In the page, only the beats that need the picture.** Chrome loads each such beat's save on the **test clock** (`?clock=fast&tpf=<N>`, TECH §15.3 to adopt): the loop runs N ticks a frame whatever the real time, and its inputs come from the same bot through `input.queue`, keyed to `clock.seq` as a replay's are (TECH §8.3). It runs to the beat, pauses, waits for `__ready`, and shoots the frame into `.run/moments/<id>/` for Claude to look at. One **real-keyboard step** per moment presses the moment's own keys by code at 1:1 (R at the shower; G, X and a digit on the line) and checks that each reached the sim as its action at its seq, so the key wiring is proved without playing the stretch at 1:1. The page part fails on any page error, console error or failed request.
- **Real time, modelled.** Neither part runs at 1:1, so a timeline's "real seconds" are the player's, computed: lived ticks at 50 ms each, every splash at its real length (TIME §6.2), and `THINK_S` 6 s for each decision taken while the clock stands still (stop motion, talk, a card) (my call: COMBAT §21's 40 to 80 decisions in 6 to 12 real minutes allow 4.5 to 18 s each, lived seconds included). The timeline prints the model's three terms, so a budget like "about 8 real minutes" is checked against the same numbers every time.
- **Pacing in every timeline** (my call, after the critics' finding that the 1:1 clock's drag was measured too late and only indirectly). Beside real and game minutes, each moment and each `recruit` sol prints:
  - **decisions per real minute** (a decision: an action, an activity begun, a card answered, a destination set, a step in stop motion);
  - **dead air**: the modelled real time spent in stretches longer than `DEAD_AIR_GAP_S` 15 s with nothing to decide and nothing new on screen, meaning no new offer (a prompt, an R, F or T offer not there before, a card, someone addressing you), no new line in the log or the ticker, no bark heard and nothing seen for the first time (shorter gaps are the rhythm of walking, not boredom; my call). The target is **dead air under 20%** of a sol's real time and of each moment's. Over it is a finding for the next patch, and the minor's bots must hold it (§2.4, item 2).
- **Budgets.** Each moment test prints its compute seconds, both parts, and fails above **60 s**. The moment suite, every milestone's moments together, stays within **6 minutes** of TECH §13.7's 10 for `npm test`, and the runner prints its total. To keep it there as moments accumulate (about 45 by 0.9), one milestone's page parts share one page and load beat saves rather than play through; if the total still passes 6 minutes, the oldest milestones' moments keep their full node run and one picture beat each.
- **Moments never expire.** The moment suite of every earlier milestone runs at every minor until 1.0. A later change that breaks one is a regression to fix, unless the user changes the design, in which case the moment is rewritten in the same change.

### 2.4 Done: the minor's checklist

Claude cuts `tools/release.sh minor` when all of these hold:
1. Every acceptance moment plays in a career from a new game and from its fixture, freshly generated (`fixtures-fresh` green); its moment test is green inside its 60 s; its printed timeline has been read and is kept in PLAYTESTS.md (§2.9).
2. The milestone's bots run their full spans with no error and print within their targets (each milestone lists them), dead air under 20% of each sol among them.
3. `npm test` is green with the heavy tests on the Mac within TECH §13.7's 10 minutes, the moment suite inside its 6, and `npm run test:quick` stays under 60 s (TECH §13.7). The soak (TECH §13.4: 10 minutes, 60 at 0.9) is run at the minor on its own, outside `npm test`, and its numbers go into PLAYTESTS.md (my call; a 10-minute soak cannot fit inside a 10-minute suite). A minor needs the heavy suite, so while the user may be playing (CLAUDE.md: quick tests only), Claude goes on cutting patches on QUICK and cuts the minor when the machine is free (my call).
4. Every bench scene of this milestone and the earlier ones holds TECH §16's budgets on the Mac at 16 px/m (the user's pick) and under the CPU throttles; the announcement offers the bench card for Windows.
5. The previous minor's edge save (or the last one written, from 0.8 on, when the career has no edge) loads and plays on (§2.6), and the chain test is green, or names the older edge it retires, with the reason in CHANGELOG.md.
6. **Every test scene of the milestone has the user's score, or the user's explicit waiver** in their own words in the chat (quoted with its date in PLAYTESTS.md), and no join is still provisional unless waived (§2.2). This is the one item that waits for the user.
7. No known crash, softlock or lost save. Every complaint the user raised during the milestone is fixed, or written into DESIGN.md as a decision in the user's words.
8. README, the design sections and every module header give the numbers the code has (CLAUDE.md).
9. CHANGELOG.md says what changed for the player and which numbers moved.

### 2.5 Releases and the play copy

- **Patches** at every round of work (CLAUDE.md), with the user's mid-play requests folded into the next one. **Minors** when §2.4 holds, Claude's to cut (the user, 2026-11-05). **Candidates and 1.0** only on the user's word (§14).
- **The announcement** of every release gives: the version; what is in it, in short; where the content edge stands; which test scenes are new and want scores, and which joins are provisional; for a minor, its acceptance moments and how to reach each; and that it is ready to promote. Nothing is promoted unasked, and a release that breaks the user's game is undone with `tools/promote.sh <previous tag>`.
- **While the user plays** (the play server answers on :8200), the machine is shared: quick tests only, and no heavy benchmarks unless asked (CLAUDE.md).

### 2.6 The content edge and the edge save

- **The edge** is the last moment a build supports. At 0.1 it is the night of the warning order's sol. On reaching it, the game waits for a point from which the save can be carried into a changed place (my call): the player **in their own rack after taps**, the rifle turned in at the armoury, kit stowed in the locker and the footlocker, no skip in flight, no fight, no reflex pending, the body steady. Lying down to sleep there writes the career to its **edge slot** (`edge`, beside `auto` and the manual slots of TECH §7.2) and shows the **edge card** in place of the night's splash: what comes next ("Echo Company embarks in the second lift. The move is OLYMPUS 0.2.") and two choices, back to the title or a new career in a new world. A player who stays up is not hurried: the edge waits for the rack, and taps is still an obligation with its consequences.
- **Carried across versions: N−1 to N, and a chain.** An edge save written on minor N loads on minor N+1 and its patches through `upgradeState`, and plays on from its exact second. That is the obligation, and only that (my call, after the critics' finding that "every minor's edge in every later version" grows without bound). The frozen fixture `tests/fixtures/edge-<N>.json` proves it (the "first fixture save for the upgrade tests" that TECH §18 puts in 0.1). **One chain test** (`save-edge-chain`, heavy, from 0.2) loads every frozen edge fixture in a single run through the current `upgradeState`, which holds every earlier upgrade in order (TECH §7.7), and steps each 400 ticks. An edge older than N−1 that no longer loads may be retired, with a CHANGELOG line saying why. Other saves from older versions load "only where it comes naturally" (the user, 2026-10-26). The edge save is the one save worth that work, because a career is the game and can hold days of play (my call; open question 3).
- **Re-placed by ids, never by coordinates.** A later minor may change the place the edge stands in: 0.2's predeploy and 0.4's dwell will likely touch Melas's templates. So `upgradeState` puts the player, their rack and their kit back by area, room and fixture id (`melas.echo.1`, `bay-2-1`, the rack and its locker), never by saved coordinates, and rebuilds the area from the newer template (TECH §7.7 and PLACES are asked for stable room and fixture ids across template versions, §18). That is why the edge waits for the rifle in its armoury rack and the kit in the locker: nothing loose lies on a floor that may have moved.
- **Patches move the edge** as joining proceeds, or fill the career before it (the 0.1.x patches fill the standby and leave the edge at the warning order), and each says where it now is. An edge save written on a patch loads on later patches where it comes naturally, and always on the next minor.
- **Death and Ironman are untouched.** The edge is not a death, and the memorial does not change. In Ironman the edge is written into the career's one slot, which is then its edge save (CAREER §23).

### 2.7 Parallel work

Test scenes are not independent: each adds bus facts, actions, keys, activities, state keys (under `people[*].body` and elsewhere), fixture kinds and buildings, and several extend the same modules (`body/mods.js` above all). CLAUDE.md lets only the lead change the shared registries. So a parallel round runs in four steps (my call, after the critics' finding that "independent by construction" would mean agents waiting on each other or merging into the same files):
1. **Pre-registration**, a lead's patch before the round: every facts name, action prefix, key, activity id, body field, state key, fixture kind and building id the round's scenes need, entered in their registries with stubs (the facts registry and the syntax rules check them, TECH §2.7), and an extension point per shared module, so each scene's contribution (its `bodyMods` contributor, its activities) is a file of its own registered in a fixed order.
2. **One agent per scene**, in its own worktree (`git worktree add <scratchpad>/<scene>`, or `.claude/worktrees/`), owning its named files and one port of 8230 to 8239, never committing (CLAUDE.md). A scene that finds it needs an entry the lead did not pre-register stops and asks rather than adding it.
3. **Merges in a fixed order**, stated in the round's brief, with the determinism suite (TECH §8.2) and the quick suite run after each merge, before the next.
4. **One release per merged scene**, or two scenes in a patch.

The first round of this kind is 0.1.x's: the Tank, the G-house, the lane and the mat, merged in that order (§5.8). In stage B of 0.0.x, the range's three slices and the bay's three are two sequential tracks that may run side by side on the same rules.

## 3. The order, and why

### 3.1 Principles (my calls)

1. **Follow the first career.** Milestones come in the order a new recruit lives them: Melas, the voyage, Europa, the way home and the dwell, the sea, recon, the Raiders, the long career. Every minor extends one career with no dead end before its edge, so the user plays forward, never sideways.
2. **Build each engine once, at the shape 1.0 needs; fill it as the places need it; grow content late.** The skip engines, the event queue, the state's domains and saves, the renderer and its budgets, perception, the gun's state machine, the body's ledgers and the check are built in 0.0.x at their full shape, with tests that pin their numbers. An engine whose first use comes later is built where it is first used, still at its full shape: the gas model with the Tank (0.1.x), acute G with the Long Arm (0.1.x), LONGHAUL's world layer with the voyage (0.2), whose prologue then rolls the world a 0.1 career lived in (§3.4). Later milestones add data, places and people on them. Where a later milestone needs an engine from LONGHAUL, its interface comes first with a stand-in source: 0.1's news board reads SETTING's authored timeline, the very one 0.2's prologue injects, and 0.2's general quarters drills feed the same timestamped interior events that 0.4's real fight will (COMBAT §13.1).
3. **Training first, then for real.** 0.1 and its patches build every combat system in training form (blanks and the engagement harness, marking rounds, the mock checkpoint, the mat; COMBAT §22), so 0.3 turns them live on proven code. 0.2's drills do the same for ship combat at 0.4.
4. **One new kind of place per milestone**, each bringing its palette, moods, frames, sounds and bench scene: the garrison (0.1), the ship under thrust and in free fall (0.2), the under-ice town and the airless surface (0.3), the ship hit in battle (0.4), the target hull in vacuum (0.5), the special-operations settings (0.6, 0.7).
5. **Measure the risks first.** What could force rework is measured in 0.0.x and 0.1: the look, the frame budget on Windows, lived-against-skipped parity, determinism, save size, the mess at noon's 160 live agents (CONTRACTS §8), and the pace of the 1:1 clock: a training sol's walking minutes on the site plan from 0.0.3, before any building is drawn (§4.1), and dead air from the first scene with people (§2.3).
6. **A base in every milestone** (the brief): the squad bay, the berthing deck, the FOB, the mess deck, the recon company's bay. The sol always ends somewhere with a shower.
7. **The gun first.** The first mechanic scene the user scores is the rifle in the hands (`test-handling`, §4.2): the brief's "Shooting should play as in Tradecraft", pillar 2, and the most-ported system, so the earliest fun is also the best-tested code.
8. **A first career soon, then a better one each patch** (my call, after the critics' finding that 0.1 as first drafted held most of the game's engines at full shape before anyone could play a career). 0.1.0 is the smallest career that is still a whole standby week; whatever the week can do without for a while is a 0.1.x patch, joined one scored scene at a time. The arrangement of 2026-11-05 (autonomy, something new each round) wants the career early.

### 3.3 Europa before ship combat (my call)

An earlier ordering put ship combat straight after the voyage and Europa after it. I put Europa at 0.3 and ship combat at 0.4, for these reasons:
- **Europa is the brief's own example** ("a drop ship to be deployed for peace keeping on Europa"), the heart of the line marine's game, and the earliest big fun after the standby.
- **The first career has no dead end.** With ship combat at 0.3, the career would stop at the arrival in Jovian space for two minors in a row.
- **Live ground combat lands on fresh code.** 0.1.x's training systems (cover, suppression, drills, the ROE lane, first aid) go live while they are new.
- **No rework.** Ship combat's port (LONGHAUL's `weapons.js`, `sensors.js`, `combatants.js`, `ordnance.js`, `damage.js`, `agents.js`: about 3,000 lines) shares nothing with Europa, and 0.2 builds its interior side with drills, so the real fight plugs in at 0.4. It then meets the player on the voyage home, and the first dwell after it opens the doors to the sea (0.5).

The alternative costs little to switch to before 0.3 starts, and none after (open question 1).

## 16. Fun

**What the road gives the user to play, and when.** Something new every few days, as tradecraft's rule gave it (there the scored tests became the tutorial, `tradecraft-design-3.md` §16; here they become the career's moments): a look to choose, then a rifle to clear, a line to shoot at 300 m, a window and a cleaning mat, a bay to live in, a squad to talk to, an inspection to pass; then a whole career at 0.1.0; then, patch by patch, a tank, a centrifuge, a lane, a mat, a patrol, a checkpoint, office hours, each scored and then found again inside that career. ## 19. Open questions for the user

Each has a default the work follows until the user answers.

1. **Europa before ship combat?** 0.3 is the Europa tour and 0.4 brings ship combat on the voyage home (§3.3). The alternative swaps them, so ship combat follows the voyage while the ship is fresh, and Europa waits a milestone. *Default: Europa first.*
2. **The score card.** Will you score the test scenes on three aspects from 0 to 100 and a note, pasted back with "Copy my scores", or would you rather give a one-line verdict in the chat? *Default: the score card. Patches never wait for it, and a join made before its score is marked provisional; the minor waits for every scene's score, or your word that it need not.*
3. **Careers across versions.** Should a career carry from one minor to the next through its edge save (the one save upgraded on purpose), while other old saves load only "where it comes naturally"? Each minor must load the previous minor's edge; one chain test tries all the older ones, and an old edge that no longer loads may be retired with a line in the changelog. *Default: yes.*
4. **The milestone brief.** Before each milestone, a short plan in the chat with work starting at once, or a pause for your word before each one (as you asked of tradecraft's 0.2)? *Default: the brief, no pause.*
5. **Tests and moments in your play copy.** Should the title of the copy you play carry the Tests menu, with the scored scenes and a direct start for every acceptance moment (worlds marked as tests, never on your profile or memorial)? *Default: yes.*
6. **What 1.0 must hold.** Everything to 0.9 (the line career, the sea, recon, the Raiders, the long career and the whole catalogue), or a smaller 1.0 after 0.5 (the line marine's career, with special operations as 1.x)? *Default: everything to 0.9.*
